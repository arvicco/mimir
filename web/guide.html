mimir — how to read this dashboard

mimir is a personal Bitcoin analytics dashboard. Four instrument families — options positioning (GEX), a multi-signal regime composite, an LPPL bubble-diagnostic suite, and a treasury-company analyser — are computed on a private machine and published as pre-built charts roughly every two hours. The dashboard is a dumb renderer: it draws whatever the last publish produced and computes nothing itself.

Reading the header

The one line at the top of the dashboard tells you how fresh the data is before you read any chart.

The dot cluster — one small dot per published data key. green means the key is fresher than its TTL (time-to-live — the age past which the data is treated as stale); amber means it has aged past the TTL but not yet three times it; red means older still. Hover any dot to see its key and last publish time (key@HH:MM, UTC).
The pub slot — pub HH:MMZ · n/N fresh is the time of the newest publish tick across all keys (Z = UTC) and how many of the N keys are currently green.
Keep-last-good — when an upstream source fails, the affected card ages; it never blanks. Its dot drifts amber then red and its age counter keeps climbing, but the last good chart stays on screen. An honest red beats a silent lie: you always know how old the number in front of you is.

GEX tabs: BTC / MSTR / TREND

What it shows — GEX (gamma exposure) is the dollar amount options dealers must re-hedge for every 1% move in the underlying. Dealers who are net long gamma sell rallies and buy dips, which pins price; net short gamma dealers do the opposite, which amplifies moves. The BTC tab buckets dealer gamma across Deribit and nine US spot-ETF option chains on one BTC-equivalent price axis; the MSTR tab shows MicroStrategy's own single-name chain on its equity price axis (deliberately not merged — MSTR gamma lives on the stock, not the coin); the TREND tab is a daily history of where spot sat relative to the flip and the walls.

How to read it — above zero is long gamma (pinning), below zero short gamma (amplifying). Three markers carry the structure: the gamma flip, the level where net GEX crosses zero (below it the market trades short-gamma — expect faster, gappier moves; distance from spot to the flip is the cushion); the call wall (CW), the largest positive strike bucket; and the put wall (PW), the most negative. Walls attract and then repel price, so they read as range edges. This is a naive dealer model (dealers assumed long calls, short puts), so trust the levels and walls more than the sign. On the TREND tab a MP Δ in the title cross-checks our flip against Coinglass's max-pain level.

The title number — GEX $M/1% · spot <price> (net dealer gamma in $M per 1% move, and spot); the TREND title carries flip distance, the current gamma-regime run length, and the max-pain delta.

Cadence — published bi-hourly. Deribit open interest is live; CBOE quotes are ~15 min delayed and OPRA open interest is previous-day. The TREND tab gains one point per day from the 08:15 GEX snapshot (options data cannot be backfilled).

When it fails — a venue with no open interest is omitted entirely, not drawn as zero. The sign convention is the weak part of the model on two-sided Deribit flow — read the flip, the walls, and the magnitude, not the long/short label.

Scenario regime composite

What it shows — seven cheap, independent signals, each scored -1 / 0 / +1 for whether the tape supports flush, base, or recovery at its own horizon: ETF flows (US spot-ETF net flows), funding/basis (perp funding, contrarian), Coinbase premium (US institutional bid), macro liquidity (Fed net liquidity and real yields), hash ribbons (miner capitulation), MVRV (market cap / realized cap), and stablecoin supply. The right column shows one coloured cell per module (red -1 / grey 0 / teal +1).

How to read it — the weighted composite (sum(weight×score) / 12) is banded FLUSH / LEAN-FLUSH / NEUTRAL / BASE / RECOVERY. No single module is trustworthy alone; the signal is the composite's drift across successive readings, which is why the main plot is the composite path over time.

The title number — Scenario <regime> ±<composite>.

Cadence — published bi-hourly; a new composite reading is appended to the history once a day (06:45). The modules lead at different horizons — funding and premium by days, flows by days to weeks, hash / MVRV / stables by weeks, macro by months.

When it fails — a dead data source scores a fail-soft 0 and marks itself unavailable — a healthy 0 and a blind 0 read the same on the strip, so a module pinned grey may be neutral or simply down. The macro module needs a FRED key to score; ETF flows is a web scrape that degrades on layout drift.

LPPL regime falsification

What it shows — the LPPL-as-regime claim holds that BTC's log price is an anti-bubble — a post-peak decline with log-periodic oscillations — around a genesis-anchored power law. The suite does not try to prove this; it runs four independently falsifiable tests daily (out-of-sample trend Bayes factor, damping envelope, anti-bubble LPPLS fit, and oscillation significance) and accumulates evidence either way, any one test able to kill the claim. The chart plots that evidence ledger over time, not price versus trend.

How to read it — three stacked panels: (top) the price/trend ratio against the damping-envelope bound (amber, last cycle's trough ratio) and floor (red, the worst historical ratio) — the ratio must hold above the bound for the damping story to survive; (middle) the trailing-1-year log10 Bayes factor of the global power law versus its best rival, with a zero line — read its sign and drift, not its absolute size; (bottom) the age-adjusted percentile Z.

The title number — LPPL <verdict> ±<composite>. The verdict vocabulary is REGIME-INTACT / SUPPORTED / INDETERMINATE / STRESSED / FALSIFIED; if either the trend or the envelope test reads -1 the verdict is capped at STRESSED, and if both do it is FALSIFIED outright.

Cadence — published bi-hourly; the tests run and the ledger is appended once a day (06:45).

When it fails — the first run bootstraps its score cache weekly, later runs densify to daily — so the Bayes-factor magnitude is not comparable across cache densities. Read the sign and the per-horizon pattern, never the raw number.

BTCo treasury-company table

What it shows — the universe of Bitcoin treasury companies — levered, reflexive BTC holders. Above BTC NAV they issue shares to buy more coin (the flywheel on); below NAV the flywheel dies and leverage can turn them into forced sellers. The card pairs a labelled bar row per company with a stress gauge and a sortable table of the raw metrics.

How to read it — mNAV is market cap over BTC NAV — the premium (>1) or discount (<1) the market puts on the coins the company holds; netNAV is the same premium measured on the equity's claim after senior debt and preferred are netted out. The per-company verdict buckets netNAV: DEEP-DISC < 0.90 · UNDER < 1.10 · FAIR < 1.45 · RICH < 1.90 · OVER. The aggregate stress score (0–100, BTC-weighted) blends the share of the universe below mNAV 1, the median-mNAV shortfall below 1.40, and aggregate leverage, banded CALM < 25 · ELEVATED < 50 · STRESSED < 75 · CRITICAL.

The title number — BTCo stress <n> <band>.

Cadence — published bi-hourly; prices, FX, and BTC spot are fetched live each run. Company fundamentals are FROZEN (development stopped 2026-08-10, pending a rethink): no new filings are ingested, and rows show STALE as their as-of dates age. Treat the fundamentals as historical, not current.

When it fails — a row flagged STALE (its BTC-as-of date is over 120 days old) or * (placeholder seed data) is not yet trustworthy. The model carries no cash or operating-business value, so diversified holders (DJT, BLSH) read richer than they are until a cash model lands — the stress score measures balance-sheet posture, not cash flow.

Volatility tabs: SURFACE / SPREAD / BASIS

What it shows — where GEX says how dealers are positioned, this card says what the market pays for volatility. SURFACE is BTC's implied-vol term structure from Deribit — ATM IV (the at-the-money level) at three tenors, plus the 25-delta risk reversal (RR25) (call IV minus put IV) and the 25-delta butterfly (FLY25). SPREAD is MSTR's ATM IV minus BTC's, tenor by tenor — the live price of treasury-company leverage. BASIS is the annualized basis of Deribit's dated futures over spot, per expiry, plus the OI-weighted perpetual funding rate.

How to read it — a negative RR25 means the market pays up for downside — puts cost more than calls (fear); a rising FLY25 means fatter tails. A positive MSTR−BTC spread is what the option market charges for MSTR's leverage right now. On BASIS, positive basis and positive funding mean the market pays to be long (contango, leverage demand); a flip negative is deleveraging or fear. These readings are descriptive only — there are no thresholds and no scores here.

The title numbers — Vol surface · ATM <tenor>d <pct>%, MSTR-BTC IV · <spread>, and Basis ann% · funding <pct>%/8h.

Cadence — published bi-hourly off live Deribit data; the surface also snapshots daily (08:15) so IV rank and percentiles become buildable after a few weeks of accumulation.

When it fails — a tenor whose option chain is too thin is dropped, not drawn as zero. The funding leg needs a Coinglass key and fails soft to a reasoned null without it.
